# Data research notes

Knowledge management for the dataset in this folder — what was used to build it, what's known to be missing, and what to check first the next time the data gets refreshed. Start here before re-researching from scratch.

**Last full research pass:** 2026-08 (all `last_verified_date` / `logo_last_verified` fields reflect this). Three passes happened this month: an initial pass (flagship-only products, top-10-states-per-bank locations), a **completeness backfill pass** triggered by a user audit (thin product coverage, truncated state coverage), and a **no-SSN pathway re-verification pass** (below) triggered by the project owner's own real-world account-opening experience. Everything below reflects all three passes as current state.

## International-student / visa-holder no-SSN pathway (2026-08 re-verification pass)

**Why this exists**: the project owner opened a Bank of America account as an international student using only a passport + F-1 visa/I-20 — no SSN, no ITIN. The Phase 2/2b research passes above had checked general no-SSN eligibility and gotten `false` for every checking/savings product across every branch-based bank except HSBC Premier — they never specifically asked "can an international student open this with just a passport," a materially different and more specific question that many banks answer differently than their general/online-flow requirements. This is a **narrower research method than general no-SSN research** — worth repeating specifically, not folded into the general refresh process below.

**Method**: search each bank's own dedicated international-student-banking pages first, then university international-student-office banking guides (these are unusually reliable — universities have direct incentive to publish accurate, current requirements for their students), then general "banking for international students" roundups only as a last resort. General no-SSN eligibility pages were NOT sufficient sourcing for this — they describe the standard/online flow, which is a different question.

**New field**: `no_ssn_requirements` (array of short strings) added to `checking_accounts.json` / `savings_accounts.json`, populated only when `accepts_no_ssn` is true and there's a specific pathway to describe (e.g. `["passport", "F-1 or J-1 visa", "I-20 or DS-2019", "in_person_application_required"]`). This is the checking/savings analog of credit cards' `eligibility_requirements`. **Deliberately does not touch `can_open_online` or `requires_branch_visit`** — those describe the bank's general/standard product; the no-SSN pathway's in-person requirement is a separate, coexisting fact captured only via the `in_person_application_required` tag. Surfaced in the UI as "Requirements to open without an SSN" (Bank Detail flip-card back face) — labeled that way specifically so it doesn't read as contradicting the general online-opening field next to it.

**Results — confirmed TRUE (real passport-only pathway, corrected `accepts_no_ssn: true`)**:
- **Chase** (`chase_total_checking`, `chase_secure_banking`, `chase_savings`) — chase.com's own international-student page + Chapman University ISS guide. In-person only.
- **Bank of America** (`bofa_advantage_plus_banking`, `bofa_advantage_safebalance_banking`, `bofa_advantage_savings`) — BofA's own Better Money Habits + international student account pages, explicitly "a U.S. TIN/ITIN is not required unless you've been issued one." Matches the project owner's real account. In-person only.
- **Wells Fargo** (`wells_fargo_everyday_checking`, `wells_fargo_clear_access_banking`, `wells_fargo_way2save`) — Wells Fargo's own ID-requirement pages carve out non-U.S. citizens without a US tax ID; corroborated by UVA and NYU Wagner international student pages. In-person only.
- **PNC Bank** (`pnc_simple_checking`, `pnc_standard_checking`, `pnc_standard_savings`) — PNC's own international-students page states explicitly that non-SSN/ITIN applicants cannot apply online and must open in-branch with passport + visa/student ID. In-person only.
- **U.S. Bank** (`us_bank_smartly_checking`, `us_bank_smartly_savings`) — U.S. Bank's own blog describes a dedicated "campus banking" team opening accounts for international students without an SSN, using country-specific foreign tax-ID documentation. Corroborated by Ohio State University's international affairs office (hosts a US Bank campus branch). In-person only.
- **Truist** (`truist_one_checking`, `truist_one_savings`) — Truist's own Bank Services Agreement documents recording passport number/issuing country in lieu of SSN for nonresident aliens; corroborated by Florida State University's international office. In-person only.
- **HSBC** (`hsbc_premier_relationship_savings`) — confirmed as an add-on to an already-no-SSN-eligible Premier checking relationship (Premier checking/savings were already correctly `true` from an earlier pass); this one product had been missed.

**Results — confirmed FALSE (real negative, left `accepts_no_ssn: false`, not a gap)**:
- **Citibank** — Citi's own official account-opening pages state SSN/ITIN is required with no documented passport-only carve-out, actively contradicting some university guides that list Citi as student-friendly. Treated as a genuine open question given the conflicting evidence, not flipped to `true` on university-guide sourcing alone when Citi's own current site says otherwise.
- **Capital One** — multiple independent sources (Capital One's own guidance, NerdWallet, Wise) confirm an ITIN must still be obtained even for in-person applications; passport alone doesn't substitute. Doesn't meet the "no SSN AND no ITIN" bar.
- **M&T Bank** — no source (not M&T's own site, not Northeast-footprint university guides, not general roundups) documents a passport-only pathway.
- **TD Bank** — TD does accept an ITIN in place of an SSN in-branch, but not passport-alone with neither — doesn't meet the "no SSN AND no ITIN" bar.
- **Ally** — Ally's own account-opening page requires SSN or ITIN with no alternative; no branches exist for in-person identity verification either way. Confirms the hypothesis that Ally's fully-digital model doesn't support this pathway.
- **Zolve, Firstcard, Nova Credit** — spot-checked, all already accurately reflected in the existing data (Zolve/Firstcard genuinely passport-only by design; Nova Credit still has no deposit/lending product of its own).

**Rollup impact**: `has_product_without_ssn` flipped from `false` to `true` for `chase`, `us_bank`, and `truist` (Bank of America, Wells Fargo, PNC, and HSBC were already `true` from earlier passes for other reasons — e.g. a secured card with `requires_ssn: false` — so no double-report there, but the underlying checking/savings evidence now also independently supports it for those four).

## Exhaustive state coverage method (`state_coverage_completeness: "exhaustive_fdic"`)

For the 11 branch-based traditional banks, every one of the 50 states + DC (51 total) was queried **individually** against the FDIC BankFind API, not aggregated from a single pull or inferred from a top-N result — every state has a real, confirmed answer (including confirmed `0`), per the three-value model below.

- **Find the CERT first**: `https://api.fdic.gov/banks/institutions?search=NAME:<bank name>&fields=NAME,CERT,STALP,ACTIVE,OFFICES&limit=10` — match on `ACTIVE:1` and a plausible `OFFICES` count (bank holding companies often have several inactive/shell CERTs with the same or similar name; the real retail bank is usually the one with `OFFICES` in the hundreds/thousands).
- **Per-state confirmed count**: `https://api.fdic.gov/banks/locations?filters=CERT:<cert>%20AND%20STALP:<state>&fields=STALP&limit=0` — read `totals.count` (a `limit=0` query still returns an accurate count without fetching individual records, much faster for 561 queries total: 11 banks × 51 states).
- **API quirk — always quote the state code**: `STALP:OR` (Oregon, unquoted) is parsed as the boolean operator `OR` by the FDIC API's query syntax and returns a 400 error, not an empty result — it does NOT fail silently as "Oregon has 0 branches." Must be `STALP:"OR"` (quoted). This bit every single bank in the first run and would have silently produced wrong data (an error response with no `totals` key, which a naive script could misread) if not caught. Watch for any other state code that happens to collide with a query-syntax keyword.
- **`api.fdic.gov` vs `banks.data.fdic.gov`**: the latter 301-redirects to the former — hit `api.fdic.gov` directly if a tool doesn't follow redirects.
- **`OFFICES` vs `OFFDOM` on the institution record**: `OFFICES` is the bank's total office count (can include non-domestic/foreign offices and territories like Puerto Rico); `OFFDOM` is domestic offices across the 50 states + DC only. The sum of the 51 per-state queries should match `OFFDOM`, not `OFFICES` — for 6 of the 11 banks it matched exactly, for the other 5 it was off by a handful (≤15, ~0.3% or less), most plausibly a snapshot-timing difference between the FDIC's `institutions` and `locations` indices, not a methodology error. `num_branches_total` in `banks.json` is set to the sum of the verified per-state counts (not `OFFICES` or `OFFDOM` copied from the institution record) — this keeps `banks.json` and `locations.json` internally consistent by construction, which was the whole point of this exercise.
- Local environment note: `curl` worked reliably against `api.fdic.gov`; Python's `urllib` failed with `CERTIFICATE_VERIFY_FAILED` on this machine (local cert store issue, not an FDIC problem) — shelling out to `curl` from Python via `subprocess` sidestepped it.

## Three-value state model (`data/locations.json`)

Every state/bank pair for an `exhaustive_fdic` bank has an **explicit** entry — `{"branches": 0, "atms": null}` for confirmed-absent, never an omission. This is deliberate: an omitted state is ambiguous ("not present" vs. "not checked"), an explicit `0` is not. Only banks tagged `state_coverage_completeness: "not_applicable_no_branches"` (`ally`, `zolve`, `firstcard`) or a future `"partial"`-tagged bank should have states legitimately missing from their `states` object — for any `"exhaustive_fdic"` bank, a missing state is a bug, not a valid "unverified" signal. `nova_credit` has no `locations.json` entry at all (not a depository institution).

## Representative fee rule (canonical — used by Browse Banks, Phase 5)

With full product catalogs, a bank's "the fee" shown on its Browse Banks card is: **the lowest `monthly_fee_usd` among that bank's `checking_accounts.json` products**, displayed as "Checking from $X/mo." Apply identically across all 15 institutions — never pick a different representative product ad hoc. If a bank has zero checking products with a non-null `monthly_fee_usd` (shouldn't happen given current data, but future-proofing), fall back to the same rule applied to savings products instead, labeled "Savings from $X/mo."

## Product catalog scope (backfill pass)

"Full personal-banking catalog" was scoped deliberately, not literally "every product a bank has ever offered":
- **In scope**: all personal checking tiers, all personal savings/high-yield deposit products, all general-purpose personal credit cards issued directly by the bank under its own brand.
- **Out of scope, on purpose**: business/commercial banking products; purely co-branded airline/hotel/retail partner cards (United, Southwest, Marriott, IHG, Disney, Amazon, Costco, Home Depot, etc. — a different product category from "which bank should I choose," not part of this project's comparison surface); CDs/certificates of deposit (not part of the checking/savings/credit_card schema); accounts explicitly restricted to minors.
- Products confirmed discontinued or closed to new applicants were explicitly excluded, not included with a caveat — e.g. Chase Sapphire Checking/College Checking, Wells Fargo Platinum card, Wells Fargo Attune card, Citi Custom Cash, Citi Rewards+ (all confirmed closed to new customers as of 2026-08). If a refresh finds one of these reopened, that's worth a note here, not a silent re-add.

## Sources used, by data type

- **Branch counts per state** (`data/locations.json`): FDIC BankFind Suite API. Query `banks.data.fdic.gov/api/locations` (or `api.fdic.gov` directly — `banks.data.fdic.gov` 301-redirects there, so if a fetch tool doesn't follow redirects, hit `api.fdic.gov` directly). Filter by institution CERT number or name, aggregate the returned branch list by state. This worked cleanly for all FDIC-insured banks with physical branches. Ally, Zolve, Firstcard, and Nova Credit have no `locations.json` entry by design (no branches / not a depository institution).
- **ATM counts** (`num_atms_total`, and per-state `atms`): essentially never publicly available at the state level, and often unreliable even at the total level (banks either don't publish it or the number that circulates online blends owned + partner-network ATMs, e.g. Allpoint/MoneyPass). Expect most `atms` fields to stay `null` indefinitely unless a bank starts publishing this directly — don't spend much time re-searching these.
- **Total assets, branch/state footprint at the whole-bank level**: most recent 10-Q/10-K or earnings release, or the FDIC institution record.
- **Product fees/rates/eligibility**: each bank's own product pages, fetched directly. Some banks render current APY/rate figures via client-side JS, so a plain fetch returns `NaN`/blank where a number should be (hit Capital One, US Bank, and PNC's savings APY pages specifically) — third-party rate-aggregator sites often disagree with each other on these, so when the official page can't be scraped cleanly, the field was left `null` rather than trusting an aggregator.
- **Logos**: official newsroom/brand-asset/press pages first (most banks have one, e.g. `media.chase.com`, `newsroom.bankofamerica.com`, `pnc.mediaroom.com`, `brandcorner.td.com`). Wikimedia Commons as fallback where no clean official asset page was found (Wells Fargo, Citibank). Actual SVG download/normalization is a separate step (Phase 3), not done during this data pass — `logo_source` only records where to get it.
- **Mobile app ratings**: Apple App Store pages fetched directly worked most of the time; Google Play pages were unreliable to fetch programmatically (blocked, 404s, or JS-rendered) — expect `mobile_app_rating_android` to be `null` more often than `_ios` for that reason, not because the rating doesn't exist.

## Known nulls and why (don't re-guess these — re-research them properly or leave null)

- All per-state `atms` values, and most `num_atms_total` — see above.
- Several savings APYs (Capital One 360 Performance Savings, US Bank Smartly Savings, PNC Standard Savings) — JS-rendered pages, conflicting third-party numbers.
- Several `foreign_transaction_fee_pct` / `apr_regular_min`/`max` on secured/no-frills cards (Citi Secured Mastercard, PNC Core/Secured, Truist Enjoy Cash, US Bank Altitude Go, TD Cash) — not disclosed as clean numbers on the official page at research time.
- `mobile_app_rating_android` for US Bank, TD Bank, Zolve, Firstcard — see Google Play note above.
- `mobile_app_rating_ios`/`android` both null for Firstcard — App Store page 404'd, third-party sources had too few reviews (<20) to trust.
- `total_assets_billion_usd`, `num_atms_total`, `num_states_present` null for Zolve/Firstcard — not applicable/not disclosed for these fintechs.
- **From the backfill pass**: several premium/relationship-tier products' APY/APR fields (Chase Private Client, BofA Relationship Banking, Wells Fargo Prime/Premier Checking, PNC Performance Select, M&T Premium Savings/Preferred Money Market, HSBC Premier Relationship Savings) — these tiers frequently don't publish a flat national rate, requiring account-level or ZIP-personalized login to see. `foreign_transaction_fee_pct` null across most PNC and several M&T cards — not disclosed on reachable pages. All three M&T unsecured Visa cards have null APR — mtb.com's live rate tables returned corrupted/truncated figures on fetch (e.g. `"14.799%–.99%"`) and third-party aggregators gave 3 mutually inconsistent ranges, so nulled rather than guessed.
- **PNC's own domain (pnc.com) was unreliable to fetch directly** during the backfill pass (~80% of direct WebFetch attempts timed out or returned garbled text) — most PNC figures were cross-verified via 2+ independent secondary sources (Bankrate, WalletHub, NerdWallet, U.S. News) instead. Same issue hit Bank of America's domain in the initial pass (HTTP 405 to direct fetch) — BofA figures came from BofA's own published Clarity Statements/PDFs plus reputable aggregators. Worth trying direct fetch first on a refresh, but don't be surprised if these two domains still need the secondary-source workaround.

If products change, these three flags must be recomputed, not hand-edited.

## Rollup recomputation logic (banks.json `has_product_*` flags)

```
has_product_without_ssn      = any checking/savings product has accepts_no_ssn == true
                                OR any credit card has requires_ssn == false
has_product_with_itin        = any checking/savings/credit_card product has accepts_itin == true
has_product_no_us_credit_history_required = any credit card has accepts_no_us_credit_history == true
```

During the 2026-08 initial pass, this logic caught two real contradictions: Bank of America's and Wells Fargo's secured/flagship cards both have `requires_ssn: false`, but their bank-level `has_product_without_ssn` had been left `false`. During the same month's backfill pass — after the product catalog roughly tripled in size (36 checking + 24 savings + 67 credit cards, 127 records total, up from ~50) — it caught **two more**: `pnc_bank` and `td_bank` both had `has_product_without_ssn` wrong (PNC and TD have cards/accounts with `requires_ssn: false` that weren't reflected at the bank level), and `td_bank`'s `has_product_no_us_credit_history_required` was also wrong. Four real contradictions caught across two passes, zero of them caught by the original per-bank research — this recomputation step is not optional. **Re-run it after any product-level edit.**

**QA lesson from the backfill merge**: one research agent wrote `"bank_id": "ally_bank"` on a new savings product instead of the correct `"ally"` — a silent typo that would have made that product invisible to every bank-scoped query (Bank Detail page, Compare, the agent's per-institution view) despite passing basic JSON validation. Caught by an orphan-reference check (every product's `bank_id` must match a real `banks.json` entry) run as part of the standard post-merge validation — **always run that check after merging any research output**, not just the rollup recomputation.

## Relationship programs, referral programs, and welcome bonuses (Phase 2d, 2026-08-29)

Added three new fields, deliberately shaped by an exploratory research pass first rather than modeled on one institution (Bank of America's "Preferred Rewards"/"BofA Rewards" was the original example and would have biased the schema toward assuming every bank has one tiered program — it doesn't):

- **`relationship_programs`** (`banks.json`, array, not a nullable single object) — bank-wide, balance/relationship-tiered benefit programs. Real programs found for 6 of 15: `bank_of_america` (BofA Rewards), `citibank` (Citi Relationship Tiers), `us_bank` (Smart Rewards), `pnc_bank` (TotalRewards), `truist` (built into Truist One Checking), `hsbc` (Premier). The other 9 correctly have `relationship_programs: []` — confirmed absent, not unresearched. **`chase` deliberately gets `[]`, not a synthesized program** — its checking-tier ladder (Total → Premier Plus → Sapphire → Private Client) is already fully represented as separate `checking_accounts.json` records; inventing a "Chase Relationship Program" name would violate the "only real, named things" rule this field exists to enforce. If Bank Detail's UI (Phase 8c/2d Step 4) doesn't visibly point to the checking-tier records when this array is empty, a user could misread "no relationship_programs entry" as "Chase has nothing like this," which isn't true — it's just structured differently.
- **`welcome_bonus_description`** (`checking_accounts.json` / `savings_accounts.json`, string or `null`, mirrors the field that already existed on `credit_cards.json`) — present on every checking/savings record, `null` when no active bonus was confirmed. These are **the most time-sensitive fields in the entire dataset** — most cite an explicit expiration date inside the text (e.g. "Offer through 2026-10-14") because the underlying promotions genuinely rotate every few months. Two offers (`us_bank_smartly_checking`, `pnc_virtual_wallet_performance_select`) were confirmed active with only ~2 days left before expiration at the time of this research pass (2026-08-29, offers expire 2026-08-31) — re-verify these first on any refresh.
- **`referral_program`** (`banks.json`) — normally a single nullable object with a `terms` array (one entry per product type only when the *same* program pays a genuinely different amount per type — confirmed real for `capital_one`'s credit-card referral terms). **`chase` is the one exception where the field is an array of two separate objects instead of one object** — verified during Step 3 research that Chase runs two independently-administered referral programs (`accounts.chase.com/raf/landing` for checking, `creditcards.chase.com/refer-a-friend` for credit cards: different domains, different reward currencies — flat cash vs. card-specific points/miles, different payout windows). Any code reading `bank.referral_program` must check `Array.isArray(...)` before assuming the object shape — this is intentional schema heterogeneity, not an inconsistency to "fix."

**Correction worth flagging explicitly**: the initial exploratory pass (Step 1) reported Capital One's referral program as paying ~$300 for a checking referral vs. $25 for a savings referral — this became the concrete proof case for why `referral_program.terms` needed to support multiple entries. Step 3's verification pass could not confirm this is still accurate: no current official Capital One page for a 360 checking/savings referral program was found, and independent sources indicate that program ended around January 2023 — the $300/$25 figures likely came from conflating the (real, current) promo-code account-opening bonus with stale historical referral terms. Capital One's `referral_program` was corrected to reflect only its confirmed-active credit-card referral program (`applies_to: ["credit_card"]`); its checking/savings referral is not currently populated. **The `terms`-array design itself is still correct and still needed** — Chase's two-program situation and the general possibility of per-type payout variance justify it independent of whether Capital One turned out to be a real example — but don't cite Capital One as "the proof case" in future discussion of this schema; it wasn't.

**Two real products were found missing from the catalog while mapping these bonuses, and have since been added** (this was a genuine Phase 2b completeness gap, not just an unmapped bonus): `mt_bank_mychoice_premium_checking` (M&T MyChoice Premium Checking, $24.95/mo, confirmed via mtb.com's own checking-accounts overview page as the bank's 4th personal checking tier — the catalog previously had only 3) and `td_beyond_checking` (TD Beyond Checking, $25/mo, confirmed via td.com — the catalog previously had Convenience/Essential/Complete but not Beyond). Both added as full records with all standard fields researched directly from their product pages (not just the bonus), `last_verified_date: "2026-08"`. `scripts/recompute_rollups.py` was re-run afterward — `num_checking_products` moved from 3 to 4 for both banks. Their `welcome_bonus_description` values are now correctly attached to these real products instead of left `null` on the nearest existing one.

## Logo sourcing (Phase 3)

- **Method**: Wikimedia Commons, queried via its public API (`commons.wikimedia.org/w/api.php`) rather than guessing file URLs — `action=query&list=search&srnamespace=6` to find candidate `File:` pages, then `action=query&prop=imageinfo&iiprop=url` on the chosen title to resolve the exact `upload.wikimedia.org` download URL. 12 of 15 institutions have a real, current logo sourced this way: chase, bank_of_america, wells_fargo, citibank, capital_one, us_bank, pnc_bank, truist, mt_bank, td_bank, hsbc, ally. Exact source file titles and URLs are recorded per-institution in `banks.json`'s `logo_source` field.
- **API etiquette**: Wikimedia's API rate-limits requests without a descriptive `User-Agent` header — send one (e.g. `"MyFirstBankResearch/1.0 (project purpose; contact email)"`) and space out requests (~2-3s) or it 429s. Also watch shell quoting when building search query strings — unescaped spaces/ampersands in a query variable will silently break the request rather than erroring clearly.
- **Picking the right file among several candidates matters**: searches often return multiple logo eras/variants (e.g. Chase's search surfaced both the retail "Chase" octagon mark and the corporate "JPMorganChase" wordmark; Citibank surfaced both the current red-arc "citi" mark and an old pre-2007 logo). Picked the current, consumer-facing brand mark each time, not the first/most-generic hit — worth the same judgment call on a refresh rather than reusing a cached "first result."
- **No logo found for 3 institutions**: `zolve`, `firstcard`, `nova_credit` — small/private fintechs with no Wikimedia presence and no public downloadable press kit found on their own sites. Replaced with a plain text wordmark SVG (`Inter Tight`, weight 600, `#1F1F1D`) matching the site's own typography, per the plan's documented fallback hierarchy (official press page → Wikimedia Commons → neutral text wordmark). Re-check on a refresh in case any of these three publishes a real brand asset later.
- **`assets/logos/_fallback.svg`**: a generic neutral bank glyph (gray, `#A3A29B`), not tied to any institution — exists as a code-level fallback if a real logo file ever fails to load at runtime, not currently referenced by any `banks.json` record.
- **Licensing note**: these are the respective companies' registered trademarks, used here for informational/comparative purposes only (consistent with the site's footer disclaimer: "All bank names, logos, and trademarks are the property of their respective owners"). Not modified beyond normal display sizing/containment.
- `td_bank.svg` is unusually large (~104KB vs. 2-6KB for the others) — a verbose, uncompressed Illustrator export, still valid and correctly rendering, just not optimized. Worth running through an SVG minifier before final ship if page weight becomes a concern, not urgent for the MVP.

## Recommended refresh process

1. Re-read this file first.
2. **State coverage** (11 `exhaustive_fdic` banks): re-run the per-state FDIC method above — 51 individually-queried states per bank, quoting every state code, summing to get `num_branches_total`. This is mechanical/scriptable, not research-agent work.
3. **Product catalogs**: re-run research groups (regroup as needed — 3 banks/group gave good depth), reusing the source list above and the in-scope/out-of-scope boundaries from "Product catalog scope" — prioritize re-checking the "business changes worth re-checking" section, since those fields are most likely to have actually changed.
4. For fields currently `null`: don't assume they're still unfindable — official pages sometimes start publishing figures they didn't before. Worth a real re-check, not a copy-paste of `null`.
5. After merging new data:
   - Run an **orphan-reference check**: every product's `bank_id` must match a real `banks.json` `bank_id` — catches typos like the `ally_bank`/`ally` one above before they silently break bank-scoped views.
   - Re-run the **rollup recomputation logic** above before writing the final `banks.json` — do this programmatically (a script that loads all 5 JSON files and applies the three formulas). Four real contradictions have been caught this way across two passes so far; expect more each time the product set changes materially.
   - For any bank whose state coverage changed: confirm `num_states_present` in `banks.json` still equals the count of non-zero states in `locations.json`.
6. Update every touched product's `last_verified_date` and, if a logo changed, `logo_last_verified`.
